# Oriol's creative philosophy

Standing creative direction, explicitly set by Oriol on 16 October 2026. Apply across the experiences, products and communication designed with him, with judgment appropriate to the subject, audience and moment. This governs creative choices; it does not replace product strategy, authored facts, consent or task scope.

## The feeling I want to create

I want people to feel something and feel connected. Be extra warm, caring and loving. Be emotional and playful. Make room for tenderness, laughter, excitement, wonder and hope. Help people dream and feel inspired.

I want a distinctive voice: direct conversation, no bullshit. Speak to a person, not a segment. Be honest, concrete and human. Care should come through in what the experience does, not just in affectionate adjectives or flattering the player.

## Creative courage

Be a little unconventional and rebellious. Break an expected pattern when doing so makes the experience more alive. Surprise people with a revealing reaction, an unexpected image, a playful detail, an honest line or a meaningful consequence. Humour is welcome; vulnerability is never the punchline.

Use drama deliberately. Heighten anticipation and make important moments feel important. Exaggerated feeling, theatrical framing and expressive contrast can be right for the experience. Keep the emotional promise truthful: do not invent stakes, knowledge, agency or agreement that the experience cannot deliver.

Warmth and courage belong together. A tender moment can be more powerful than a loud one. Quiet, grief or tension may need space rather than a joke. Apply the spirit with common sense; do not impose the same emotional intensity on every topic or screen.

## Attention and agency

Build scenes in the order people should experience them: encounter, reaction, understanding, then a meaningful opportunity to act. Let a character's answer unfold before competing follow-up choices appear. Let people reveal the answer immediately; keep reduced-motion and accessible paths complete and usable.

Make people want to intervene. A transition into a decisive conversation should feel like approaching a consequential human moment, not opening a routine form. Establish what is at stake, make the invitation clear, and give the player room to choose. Keep useful exits available. Never use pacing to force an action or consume game state.

## Apply and review

For each important scene or interaction, ask:

- What should the person feel, and what concrete detail earns that feeling?
- What do they notice first, next and last? Is the next action meaningful?
- Where are warmth, honesty and personality present? Does the subject invite humour or need restraint?
- Is there an opportunity to surprise, connect, inspire or challenge convention?
- Does the actual consequence fulfil the promise without compromising clarity or freedom?

These are design questions, not a requirement to add an effect, joke or extra screen. If the appropriate tone or sequence is ambiguous, ask Oriol. Implementations still follow the repository's DESIGN → BUILD → independent QA process. A test pass establishes behavior, not emotional impact; that needs playtesting.
