## Logline

A top-performing sales director is finally facing real consequences for
years of harm nobody ever made stick — at the exact moment it becomes
convenient for someone else to get rid of him. Both things are true. The
player has to deliver a verdict that holds both at once, face to face with
the person it's about.

*(Revised per Story Critic v1: "documented harm" contradicted the case's own
thesis — the harm was specifically informal, repeatedly closed without
action, never properly documented. Fixed.)*

## Player role & frame

You're the HR Business Partner with actual sign-off authority on this PIP —
company policy requires HRBP review before a performance action against a
people-leader is finalized, and that review is a real decision, not a
recommendation upward. The case is triggered by the Chief People Officer
flagging that the timing looks off and asking for this review specifically
— a one-time inciting event, not an ongoing authority above the player. They
don't reappear, and the document no longer implies they should.

*(Revised: v1 left the player's actual authority — and the "someone senior"

## Secrets, in layers

1. Surface: Marcus claims this is a hit job.
2. Underneath: the harm is real — independently corroborated, predates this
   moment entirely.
3. Deeper: the PIP's *timing* is opportunistic — Priya's own motive, tied
   to the client-loss blame-shift.
4. Deepest: the company itself buried this three years ago. The system
   finally "holding him accountable" is the same system that let it happen.

## Twist order, as played

timing is opportunistic anyway → the company buried this itself, years ago.

## Verdict axes

1. **processLegitimacy** — `retaliatory / mixed-motive / legitimate`
   (factual, should resolve to mixed-motive with good investigation).
2. **harmSeverity** — `overstated / real-and-serious / exceeds-even-this-PIP`
   (factual, should resolve to real-and-serious).
3. **finalCall** — `proceedWithPIP / blockAndReset / modifyProcess`. This is
   the categorical choice that actually happens — it's what drives the
   confrontation scene (see below), a real branch, not flavor. Separately,
   the existing closing-statement step (the full-screen ritual already
   built for Case 002, reused here) is where reasoning quality gets graded
   — on whether the player's stated reasoning actually accounts for *both*
   factual axes and the `finalCall` they made, not as a second hidden input
   that also silently drives the scene. Two distinct things, two distinct
   jobs: `finalCall` branches the game, the closing statement scores how
   well the player can explain it.

   *(Revised: v1 said this axis was "graded on reasoning quality, not which
   option" while also saying it drives the confrontation scene by its
   categorical value — two data sources claimed to drive the same outcome
   with no stated relationship. Fixed by separating what branches the story
   from what's graded.)*

4. **escalateComplicity** — a real, player-controlled choice (not a
   designer default) about whether to formally surface the old complaint
   file, independent of Marcus's personal outcome. Has real weight, not
   just a one-line epilogue: escalating is the case's one explicit
   acknowledgment that the institution itself is culpable — the Reveal
   gets a genuinely distinct narrative branch here (not just a sentence),
   matching the thematic weight the Learning section claims this idea
   carries. Declining to escalate is also a real, defensible choice
   (protects Theo and Dana from being dragged into a bigger process they
   never asked for) — not a worse ending, a different one.

   *(Revised: v1's "default: yes... one-line epilogue" language made this
   read as a fixed coda dressed up as an axis, directly undercutting the
   case's own stated thesis that the systemic failure is the deepest,
   most important secret. Fixed by giving it real player control and real
   narrative weight.)*

## The confrontation scene (Verdict → Reveal bridge)

Not a static reveal screen — a live, freeform `WitnessChat`-style scene with
Marcus, combining two already-proven primitives in a new way: the dynamic
`buildRevealNarrative()` pattern (so it reflects the player's actual play
state) and the real witness-conversation engine (so Marcus can genuinely
react, push back, or surprise the player). His opening line and available
reactions are shaped by `finalCall` **×** his accumulated demeanor going in
(see the table below for exactly how) — the same verdict lands differently
depending on how he was treated getting there.

**Resolved — freeform, with a scene-specific validation rule** *(this was
the single most important open item per the critic; deciding it now rather
than leaving it open)*: stays fully freeform, consistent with the rest of
the case's conversation system, rather than switching to authored/bounded
choices for the climax alone. The risk the critic named — that this is the
highest-stakes scene and freeform generation might fail to hold both truths
at once — gets addressed the same way the engine already handles risk
elsewhere: a dedicated validation check, specific to this scene, run on
every generated reply before the player sees it. If a reply reads as purely
exonerating Marcus OR purely condemning him without acknowledging the other
side, it's rejected and replaced with an authored fallback line that does
hold both — the same "pragmatic safety net, not proof of semantic
correctness" pattern the existing leak-marker validation already uses, just
with a rule specific to this scene's one non-negotiable requirement.

## Learning / reflection intent

Real accountability and a corrupt process can be true at the same time —
"he deserved it, who cares how" and "the process was dirty, so he's
cleared" are both the easy, wrong move. Institutions protect their most
valuable people until protecting them stops being convenient, and the
moment accountability finally shows up is often evidence of that failure,
not proof the system works.

## Pacing / structure

*(Added per critic finding — v1 had no stated sequencing or session-length
estimate at all, which made pacing genuinely unassessable.)*

Rough shape, not a hard script — investigation stays open-form like Case
002 (no clock):

1. **Opening** (5 witnesses available/discoverable, 2 evidence items free)
   — Marcus, Priya, Sam, Theo all reachable immediately; E05 and E03 free.
   Natural first moves: hear Marcus's and Priya's opening accounts, notice
   E03's timing tell.
2. **Middle** (the pattern emerges) — Theo opens the lead to Dana; E02 and
   E06 come in; the board starts showing the harm is real, independent of
   motive.
3. **Turn** (the opportunism lands) — E04 (via Sam's trust threshold)
   confirms the timing wasn't clean either.
4. **Late** (the gut-punch) — E01 gates in, once E06 is already known;
   Sam's trust in leadership cracks if relayed.
5. **Close** — verdict (4 axes + closing statement) → the confrontation
   scene → Reveal.

Estimated session length: comparable to Case 002 (a full investigation
with 5 witnesses, 6 evidence items, no forced pacing) — not shorter, since
nothing here is skippable the way Ascent's clock lets some threads go
unexplored.
