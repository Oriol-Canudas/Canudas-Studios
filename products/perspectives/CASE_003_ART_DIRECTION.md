# "Untouchable" — Art Direction

Checked against the existing house style (`public/portraits/*`, `public/scenes/*`
for both Case 002 and Ascent) before writing this, not invented from scratch.

## The house style — what's constant across cases

- Photoreal cinematic portraiture, never illustrated/painted.
- Lighting is always environmentally motivated by the case's real world —
  Case 002's dark interrogation room, Ascent's overcast alpine light — never
  a generic studio setup.
- Direct, engaged gaze at camera on the neutral/composed portrait; demeanor
  variants shift expression and micro-posture while keeping the same
  identity, framing, and wardrobe (same "actor," same outfit, same setup).
- Naturalistic, unretouched skin and expression detail — not glamour
  photography.
- Muted/desaturated grading with exactly one accent color doing the work
  (warm light pool in Case 002; the red jacket zipper in Ascent).
- Scenes (not portraits) carry the evidence/location mood: shallow depth of
  field, a few real objects in sharp focus against a dim environment.

## "Untouchable"'s translation of that style

**World**: a modern corporate office, after hours — the PIP review genuinely
happens in off-hours, quiet-building tension. Conference rooms, glass walls,
city lights through windows at night, laptop glow, a half-empty open-plan
floor behind frosted glass. This is the case's equivalent of Case 002's
interrogation room and Ascent's mountain — the environment IS the mood.

**Palette**: cool blue-grey corporate interiors (monitor glow, fluorescent-
adjacent but dimmed) with one warm accent per shot — a desk lamp, a laptop
screen's warm-white glow, a wood-veneer conference table catching light.
Same "one accent against a muted field" rule as the existing cases.

**Portrait aspect/setup**: square, matching Case 002's convention (closer,
more interrogation-adjacent than Ascent's environmental portrait aspect) —
this case is about a room and a conversation, not a landscape.

## Character visual direction

- **Marcus** — early-to-mid 40s, used to being the most confident person in
  any room, status visibly eroding under pressure: an expensive dress shirt,
  top button undone, tie loosened or missing by his later demeanor states.
  Composed = still performing total control. Defensive = jaw set, leaning
  forward. Resigned = the one state that should look genuinely different —
  less composed, less armored, like the performance has run out.
- **Priya** — late 30s, sharp blazer, exact posture, someone who has never
  let a room see her improvise. Composed = flawless. Defensive (once E01
  lands) = the first visible crack — should read as more unsettling than
  Marcus's, because it's rarer for her.
- **Dana** — mid 30s, dressed for wherever she works now, visibly not
  part of this company's world anymore — the calm of someone who's already
  left and has nothing left to protect.
- **Theo** — late 20s, still in this office's world, dressed like someone
  trying not to be noticed right now. Nervous/shaken states should be the
  most visible of the cast — he has the most to lose by being seen at all.
- **Sam** — early 40s, mid-level, the most "normal," least stylized of the
  five — deliberately the calibrated, ordinary voice of the case; wardrobe
  and lighting should be the least dramatic of the set, on purpose.

## Scenes needed (matching Case 002/Ascent's `home`/`evidence`/`verdict` set)

- `home.jpg` — the case's opening image: an empty conference room at night,
  a PIP folder closed on the table, city lights behind glass.
- `evidence.jpg` — the evidence-table equivalent: a laptop open to an email
  thread, a printed complaint file, a phone with the overheard-remark
  message visible — same "a few real objects in sharp focus" grammar as the
  knife/photos/phone in Case 002's evidence scene.
- `verdict.jpg` — the room where the confrontation scene happens: two chairs
  facing each other, not a desk-and-visitor-chair power arrangement — a
  deliberate framing choice, since this verdict gets delivered face to face,
  not pronounced from behind authority.
